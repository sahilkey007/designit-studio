Where online retail actually loses money

⚠

The product page doesn't answer the objection

Shoppers leave a PDP because a specific question went unanswered — sizing, materials, delivery timing, returns. The page presents what the business wants to say rather than what the buyer needs resolved before committing.

☷

Checkout asks too much, too early

Account creation before guest checkout, address before shipping cost, surprise fees at the final step. Each is a defensible internal decision and each measurably costs orders — abandonment concentrates on exactly these moments.

▣

Mobile is the majority and the afterthought

Most sessions are mobile; most stores are designed and reviewed on a desktop. Thumb reach, form input, image zoom and payment-sheet integration are where mobile conversion is won or quietly lost.

↻

Acquisition is subsidising a leaky funnel

Paid spend rises to compensate for a conversion rate nobody has interrogated. Fixing the funnel is almost always cheaper per incremental order than buying more traffic to pour into it.

What you actually get

⚲

Funnel diagnostic

Analytics and session review to locate exactly which step loses the most revenue, so design effort is spent where the money actually is rather than where the page looks worst.

▦

Product detail page design

PDPs structured around the objections that stop purchase — imagery, specification, sizing, delivery, returns and social proof, sequenced by what buyers ask rather than by what is easy to populate.

→

Cart and checkout redesign

Guest checkout, transparent costs shown early, minimal field counts, address autocomplete and native payment-sheet integration. The highest-leverage surface in most stores.

◫

Discovery and navigation

Category structure, filtering and search designed so shoppers reach a relevant product quickly — the step before the funnel that determines how much enters it.

✎

Post-purchase and retention

Order tracking, returns and re-order experiences. Retention design is consistently underinvested relative to acquisition, despite a far better return per hour of work.

◯

Accessible, mobile-first design

WCAG 2.1 AA as standard and mobile as the primary design target rather than a responsive afterthought — both of which correlate directly with conversion on real device mixes.

How the engagement runs

01

Diagnose

Weeks 1–2. Funnel analysis and heuristic review to identify and size the biggest leaks. Delivered as a ranked list before any redesign is proposed.

02

Prioritise

Week 2. Issues scored on revenue impact against build effort, so the work sequences by return rather than by enthusiasm.

03

Design

Weeks 3–7. Redesign of the highest-impact surfaces, mobile-first, with every state specified including out-of-stock, partial availability and payment failure.

04

Measure

Post-launch. Against the conversion metric agreed at the start. Retail is one of the few categories where design impact is unambiguously measurable, so we commit to it.

Forced account creation, shipping costs revealed late, and excessive form fields are the three most common and most fixable causes of abandonment.
