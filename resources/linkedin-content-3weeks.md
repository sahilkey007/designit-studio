The math on fixing design isn't soft ROI. It's straightforward arithmetic.

What UX debt are you sitting on right now?

### Wednesday — Case Study Snippet
We redesigned Kelp Global's CRM dashboard.

Not because it was ugly — it wasn't. But their sales team was spending 40+ minutes/day navigating between 7 screens to complete a single client update.

Our approach: map every task the sales rep performs → identify the 3 critical flows → collapse them into one unified view.

Result: average task time dropped from 40 minutes to 11 minutes per rep.

At 15 reps, that's 435 minutes recovered daily. 7+ hours. Per day.

Design isn't decoration. It's operational efficiency.

If your team has workarounds they've "just gotten used to" — that's worth a conversation.

### Friday — Behind the Scenes
Something most design agencies won't admit:

The brief is never right the first time.

Every project we've shipped — Adda247, Betacrew, Omniyat, Kelp — the brief that came in was not the brief we designed to.

Not because clients don't know their product. But because articulating UX problems in words is genuinely hard. Most people feel the problem before they can describe it.

Our first call is always 70% listening. Not pitching. Not presenting credentials.

Just: what frustrates your users? What do they say in support tickets? Where do you see drop-off in analytics?

The best briefs emerge from that conversation. They're never sent over in a Google Doc.

## WEEK 2

### Monday — Contrarian Take
Unpopular opinion: most redesigns are unnecessary.

Before you spend ₹3–8 lakh on a product redesign, run a conversion audit first.

In 60–70% of audits I've done, the issue isn't "the design is old." It's 3 specific moments where users drop off — usually onboarding step 2, a confusing pricing page, and a checkout/signup form with too many fields.

Fixing those 3 things costs 15–20% of a full redesign.

A full redesign is often the most expensive way to solve the problem.

The right order: audit → identify the 3 critical failure points → fix those → measure → then decide if a full redesign makes sense.

#ConversionOptimisation #B2BSaaS #ProductDesign

### Wednesday — Process Post
How we scope a product design project in 20 minutes:

1. Map every user type (1 user ≠ 1 role)
2. List every task each role needs to accomplish
3. Identify which 5 tasks are highest-frequency
4. Estimate screens: (tasks × states × roles) × 1.4
5. Add 20% for edge cases and empty states
6. Add 15% for design system components

Most agencies estimate by "number of screens" alone. That misses states, empty states, error flows, responsive variants, and component work.

A screen that says "20 screens" could mean 20 static mockups or 80 states. That's a 4× difference in actual work.

Next time you get a design quote, ask: "Does this include all states, empty states, and responsive variants?" The answer tells you everything.

### Friday — Insight Post
The single highest-ROI design investment for a SaaS company at Series A:

Not a redesign. Not a mobile app.

A design system.

Here's why:

Before a design system: every new feature takes 3–5 days to design from scratch. Inconsistent UI across the product. Developer asks "what's the spacing here?" 20 times per feature.

After a design system: new screens take hours, not days. Every component already designed, documented, and in Figma. Developers have specs. Design reviews focus on logic, not pixel-pushing.

The ROI compounds with every engineer and designer you hire.

We've seen teams with 80-component design systems ship features at 3× the speed of teams without.

The best time to build one: when you have 5+ engineers. The second-best time: now.

## WEEK 3

### Monday — Question Post
If you could fix one thing in your product's UX this quarter — what would it be?

I ask every founder this in our first conversation. The answers cluster into 5 themes:

1. Onboarding — "users drop off before they see value"
2. Navigation — "people can't find what they're looking for"
3. Data density — "the dashboard is overwhelming"
4. Mobile — "nobody uses it on mobile, but we need them to"
5. Empty states — "new users see blank screens and don't know what to do"

Interesting: the #1 answer is almost always onboarding. And it's also the one most teams deprioritise because "we need to ship features first."

That's the trap. If users don't get to the value, features don't matter.

What's your #1?
