Score your website before you redesign it

Before you spend on a redesign, find out which parts of your website actually hold visitors back. Answer each check and get a score and a short list of where to start.

How do you know if your website needs a redesign?

A website needs a redesign when its structure stops it explaining, convincing and converting: visitors cannot tell what you do, cannot find proof, or cannot see a sensible next step. Fixing copy or a single page is often enough when the structure is sound.

The checklist: 30 checks

Answer each check for your product as it is today. Your score and a short start-here list update as you go.

1. First impression and clarity

Can a first-time visitor tell what you do, and who it is for, from the first screen?

Does the headline describe an outcome the visitor cares about rather than your internal category name?

Is there one primary call to action above the fold?

Is it clear what happens after the visitor clicks it?

2. Structure and navigation

Is the navigation organised around what visitors want to do rather than around your org chart?

Can a visitor reach any key page (services or product, engagement or pricing, work, contact) in two clicks?

Do page titles and headings tell visitors where they are?

Does every page end with a sensible next step rather than a dead end?

3. Proof and trust

Is proof (named clients, case studies, reviews, security) placed next to the claim it supports?

Are case studies specific about the problem, what was done and what changed?

Can visitors see who is behind the company: people, location and contact details?

Are claims specific and checkable rather than superlatives?

4. Conversion and forms

Does each page offer a next step matched to the visitor's stage: learn, compare or talk?

Do forms ask only for what you need to respond?

Do button labels describe the outcome rather than the mechanism?

Does the visitor know what happens after submitting, and how soon you will reply?

Is there a low-commitment option for visitors who are not ready to talk?

5. Content

Does the copy explain how things work rather than only describing them?

Are the questions buyers ask (cost drivers, timelines, process) answered on the page?

Is the text easy to scan, with clear headings, short paragraphs and meaningful lists?

6. Mobile

Does every key page work at phone width without horizontal scrolling?

Are tap targets large enough, with the main action easy to reach?

Are forms usable on a phone, with the right keyboards and autofill?

7. Speed and stability

Does the main content appear quickly on a mid-range phone on mobile data?

Does the page stay still while it loads, with no jumping text or buttons?

Are images sized and compressed for the screens they appear on?

8. Accessibility and findability

Is text readable for everyone, with enough contrast and size?

Can the whole site be used with a keyboard alone?

Does each page have a unique, descriptive title and meta description?

Are important pages linked from other pages, not only from the menu?
