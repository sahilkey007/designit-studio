# Dribbble and Behance copy for all 18 case studies

Everything below comes from what is already published on designit.co.in. No new numbers, awards or client claims. The few figures used (3M+ students, 375K users, 12-minute sessions, 500 users surveyed, 25 badges) are the ones already on the site.

**Before you post**

- Check that each client is fine with the work going on public portfolios. The evidence ledger still says "client approval: unknown" for Adda247, Kelp, Tata Elxsi and Omniyat/R-Centric. Truenorth (a fund manager's investor portal) is the one I would ask about first.
- Behance and Dribbble copy is written differently on purpose. Don't paste the same text on both, and don't paste the website text either. Duplicate text on three places is the part that looks machine-made and costs you in search.
- Read each one out loud once. If a sentence sounds like you wouldn't say it, change it. A few small edits in your own voice do more than any trick.
- Tag limits: Behance 14, Dribbble 20. I kept every list inside the limit. Keywords are the terms designers and hiring teams actually use when tagging and searching this kind of work. I have not measured search volume.
- Every Behance description ends with a link back to the case study on the site. Dribbble links are nofollow, so the main value there is traffic and the profile, not SEO.
- Cover image for each one is listed. Those are the files already in `assets/projects/`.

## ADDA247 (EdTech)

### 1. Homepage Revamp

**Cover:** `assets/projects/adda247/homepage-revamp-hero.webp`

**Behance title:** Adda247 App Home Screen Redesign | EdTech UX Case Study

**Behance description:**
Adda247 is an exam-prep app used by 3M+ students, and its home screen had turned into one long feed. People scrolled for a few seconds and left.

The numbers told the story. Study material was the most clicked thing in the whole app, but you needed extra taps to reach it. 375K users, 57.6% of the base, were opening the hamburger menu just to find their study sections. Meanwhile the Daily Quiz, one of the most used features, was easy to miss.

So we rebuilt the home around three things students actually do: Watch, Practice and Read. Quizzes and current affairs moved up, banner ads stopped interrupting the feed, and free users get a proper way to try things before anyone pushes them to a purchase.

We worked from a first-time user persona (Yash, 17, preparing for defence exams), mapped his journey and its low points, then tested layouts in rounds. iOS and Android. Research, information architecture, UI, prototype and handoff.

Full process, persona and journey map: https://designit.co.in/projects/adda247/homepage-revamp/

### 2. Live Class Module

**Cover:** `assets/projects/adda247/live-class-hero.webp`

**Behance title:** Live Class UX for an EdTech App | Real-Time Learning Interface

**Behance description:**
Live classes on Adda247 used to run on third-party streaming tools. Students hopped between apps, the platform had no say over quality, and average sessions lasted 12 minutes in classes that ran 60 to 90. People joined, watched a bit and left.

We designed an in-house live class module inside the app itself. The video stays the main thing, and everything else (chat, polls, doubts, shared files) comes in only when it's needed, so a small phone screen never feels crowded. When a teacher launches a poll, the poll panel opens. When a doubt is being answered, the Q&A takes over.

We also designed for weak connections, because that's what most students have. And recordings keep the chat, polls and shared material with the video, so someone who missed the class gets nearly the same thing.

Before designing anything we interviewed 30 teachers and 100 students. Mobile first, with tablet and desktop layouts.

Case study: https://designit.co.in/projects/adda247/live-class/

### 3. My Content

**Cover:** `assets/projects/adda247/my-content-hero.webp`

**Behance title:** Adda247 My Content: A Study Library Redesign | EdTech UX

**Behance description:**
Students on Adda247 buy courses, test series and PDFs as separate packages, and the app showed them exactly that way: by package. If you studied for SSC CGL and owned five of them, you hunted through five places to revise one topic.

Web app. UI/UX, information architecture and content strategy.

### 4. Studio App for Smart Boards

**Cover:** `assets/projects/adda247/studio-hero.webp`

**Behance title:** Smart Board Teaching App UI | Large-Screen Touch UX

**Behance description:**
Adda247's classroom studios had smart boards, but most teachers used them like a plain whiteboard. After each class the diagrams, formulas and explanations were wiped, and students who missed the session had nothing to go back to.

### 6. Community Module

**Cover:** `assets/projects/adda247/community-hero.webp`

**Behance title:** Study Community Feature for an EdTech App | Gamification UX

**Behance description:**
Exam prep is lonely. Students on Adda247 would download the app, explore for a few weeks, then slowly stop opening it. Nothing pulled them back.

We designed a community inside the app with three parts. A Q&A forum where answers get voted on and experts can verify them. Small study groups for people preparing for the same exam, with shared daily goals. And a recognition layer: reputation points, 25 badges tied to real expertise (a "Reasoning Expert" badge means more than a badge for ten posts), weekly leaderboards and levels.

The hard part was keeping it useful, not noisy. Good answers rise with larger cards and a "Verified Answer" badge, and community managers get flagging workflows for moderation.

Android and iOS.

## TATA ELXSI (automotive HMI)

### 17. Infotainment System

**Cover:** `assets/projects/tata-elxsi/infotainment-hero.webp`

**Behance title:** Car Infotainment UI Design | Automotive HMI Case Study

**Behance description:**
A car screen has a harder brief than a phone. Drivers expect what their phones give them, but they have a glance and a tap to spare. Too simple and the system feels dated. Too complex and it's a distraction on the road.

We started from the safety standards: NHTSA's visual-manual guidelines, which cap a glance at 2 seconds and a task at 12. We looked at how BMW iDrive, Tesla and Mercedes MBUX handle the same problem, then set an interaction budget for every feature. Essential controls (volume, navigation, climate) are always on the home screen, everything else is exactly one tap away, and touch targets are large, at least 44px. The interface simplifies at highway speed and opens up when the car is stationary. It's dark-mode first, to cut glare at night.

We validated it with driving simulator testing. In-vehicle centre display. HMI design, interaction design and prototyping.
