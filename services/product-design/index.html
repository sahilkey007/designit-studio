What product design solves

Product design decides what a digital product should do, how it is structured and how it behaves, so that people can get real work done with it and the business can keep growing it.

It is more than drawing screens. The work covers defining what should be built, researching who it is for, structuring how it fits together, designing the interface, prototyping to test assumptions, and supporting the engineers who build it.

Most expensive product mistakes are made before anyone opens a design tool. When the business goal is clear but the product is not, starting in Figma is how teams spend six months building the wrong thing well.

Product discovery: deciding what to build

Product discovery is the research and definition work that establishes which problem a product must solve, for whom, and how you will know it worked, before design or engineering commit to a solution.

We run stakeholder interviews and user research, review analytics and the competitive landscape, and finish with a written problem definition that both sides sign off. If we disagree about the problem, this is where it surfaces, while it is still cheap.

Discovery can run on its own for two to three weeks. You get research findings, a problem definition and a recommended direction: enough to decide whether to proceed, with whom, and at what scope.

Product strategy: structure before screens

When everything on the roadmap is a priority, nothing is. Without a structural view of the product, each new feature makes the next one harder to add and the whole thing harder to use.

So the decisions we spend most time on are architectural: how the product is organised, what belongs together, which flows are primary and which are exceptions. Those decisions constrain everything downstream, which is why they get the most attention and the earliest user testing.

Some products are complex because the work is complex: regulated flows, safety-critical interfaces, dense operational tools. Simplifying is not an option there. Making the complexity legible is the actual job.

Product design for different stages

The same discipline, applied differently depending on where the product is.

New product

Define before you design

Discovery, problem definition and a validated direction, so the first build tests the right idea. See MVP design.

MVP

The smallest product that proves the point

The core flow designed properly, everything else deliberately deferred, with foundations that will not need throwing away.

Growth

Fix adoption, then scale

Activation, navigation and consistency work, plus the design system that lets more people ship without fragmenting the product.

Redesign

Change what is broken, keep what works

Start from evidence of where users struggle, not from what looks dated. See product redesign.

Enterprise

Modernise without stopping the business

Legacy workflows mapped before they are changed, migration sequenced, power users protected. See legacy modernisation.

Our process

A defined product initiative typically runs eight to twelve weeks end to end.

  1. Define (weeks 1 to 2)

    Research, stakeholder alignment and a written problem definition. Disagreements about the problem surface here, cheaply.

  2. Structure (weeks 2 to 4)

    Product architecture, flows and low-fidelity direction, validated with users before visual work starts.

  3. Design (weeks 4 to 10)

    High-fidelity design and prototypes, reviewed weekly, with system components captured as they emerge.

  4. Ship (ongoing)

    Handover, engineering support and design QA against the built product.

How much does product design cost?

Product design is quoted against a written scope rather than a day rate, and the price is driven by scope and risk more than by screen count.

What moves the price: how much research is needed; the number of platforms (web, iOS, Android, embedded); the complexity of the workflows and permission models; whether a design system is part of the work; how many iteration and validation rounds the risk justifies; and how much implementation support your team wants.

Work is usually run as a fixed-scope project, with a retainer afterwards if you want ongoing design capacity. Discovery can be bought on its own to de-risk a larger decision. International engagements are quoted in USD.
