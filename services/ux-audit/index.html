When a UX audit is the right call

⚠

Traffic is fine, conversion isn't

Acquisition spend is working and the funnel still leaks. You suspect the problem is on the page rather than in the campaign, but nobody can point at the specific step that loses people.

☷

Support keeps answering the same question

The same three tickets arrive every week. That is a design problem being absorbed by humans, and it is measurable — reducing it was worth a 60% ticket drop for Adda247.

▣

Everyone has an opinion, nobody has evidence

Design decisions are being decided by whoever argues hardest. An external audit replaces opinion with observed behaviour and gives the team a shared, ranked list to work from.

↻

You're about to spend on a rebuild

Before committing six figures to a redesign, it is worth knowing which problems are actually costing money. Often a targeted fix list gets most of the value at a fraction of the cost.

What you actually get

⚲

Heuristic and task-based review

We walk your core user journeys as your users do — signup, activation, the primary workflow, checkout or upgrade — and record every point of friction with a screenshot and an explanation.

▦

Analytics and session evidence

Where you have GA4, Hotjar, Clarity or similar, we tie observed friction to actual drop-off data, so each finding is backed by a number rather than an assertion.

→

Prioritised issue register

Every finding scored on user impact, revenue impact and implementation effort — so the list sorts itself into quick wins and structural work instead of arriving as an undifferentiated pile.

◫

Annotated recommendations

Not just what is wrong, but what to do instead — with wireframes or visual direction for the highest-impact fixes so engineering is not left interpreting prose.

✎

Accessibility check

A WCAG 2.1 AA pass over the audited journeys, flagging issues that create both legal exposure and real usability problems for a meaningful share of your users.

◯

Walkthrough session

A live session with your product and engineering team to talk through the findings, argue with them, and agree what actually goes into the next sprint.

How the engagement runs

01

Scoping

Days 1–2. We agree which journeys are in scope and which metric matters most. You get a written scope with a fixed price before any work starts.

02

Review

Days 3–7. Heuristic evaluation, task walkthroughs, analytics review and accessibility pass across the agreed journeys.

03

Prioritisation

Days 8–10. Every issue scored and ranked. This is the part most audits skip, and the part that makes the output usable.

04

Handover

Days 11–14. Written report, annotated recommendations, and a live walkthrough with your team. The deliverable is a backlog, not a PDF that gets filed.
